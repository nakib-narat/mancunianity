The experience

A unique walking experience

An easy, step-free quarter-mile audio-theatre walk through extraordinary stories, followed by a seated 15-minute riverside concert. The guided walk and concert last approximately 60-75 minutes. Please arrive 20 minutes early, so allow about 90 minutes in total.

Memories. Music. River. The city we share.

A new audio-theatre adventure for all ages

Step back in time and follow the River Irwell through live storytelling, music, historical voices and an original soundscape. Created with older residents and performed for everyone.

What happens?

Arrive at Aviva Studios

Arrive 20 minutes early for a warm welcome, check-in, headset fitting and route briefing. Walks leave promptly.

Step towards the River Irwell

Follow safe, maintained, pedestrian-only paths through a landscape of bridges, waterways and city views.

Listen through wireless headphones

Hear an original soundscape, music, field recordings, local voices and remarkable stories about people, buildings and animals.

Notice and discover together

Take part in gentle family prompts, conversation and observation. Participation is friendly and optional.

Finish with a riverside concert

Enjoy 15 minutes of live music on the steps overlooking the Irwell: a sea shanty, Manchester and Salford songs and a group singalong.

Return to Aviva Studios

Return for toilets, refreshments, a warm dry space and time to continue the conversation.

Environmental learning

The Irwell: power, damage and recovery

The River Irwell has carried people, goods, industry, waste, wildlife, memories and change through Manchester and Salford.

Industrial growth brought invention and global connections, but also severe pollution. Environmental improvements later helped wildlife return, while pollution, runoff, habitat pressure, litter and extreme rainfall remain continuing concerns.

On Boxing Day 2015, prolonged rainfall on saturated ground drove rivers across Greater Manchester to record levels. Around 750 properties were flooded internally in Lower Broughton. At the Manchester Racecourse gauge, the Irwell reached 5.67 metres, about 1.3 metres above any previous reading in 75 years of recording. At the Mark Addy pub, floodwater eventually rose above the bar.

The river's story is not a simple journey from a damaged past to a perfect present. It is a continuing story about climate, biodiversity, public access, planning, resilience and how two cities live with water.

Sources: Greater Manchester Boxing Day 2015 Flood Investigation Report; Manchester Evening News report on the Mark Addy.

Connecting blue and green spaces

A shared environmental vision with CyanLines

Mancunianity Flows shares CyanLines' belief that Manchester and Salford's rivers, canals, parks, green spaces and walking routes should be celebrated, protected and made easier for people to discover.

CyanLines is developing a vision for more than 100 miles of connected parks, waterfronts, green corridors and traffic-free walking and wheeling routes. We are delighted that Mancunianity Flows will be shared through CyanLines' public communications and newsletters.
