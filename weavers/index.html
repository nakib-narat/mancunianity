People who weave the project together

The Weavers

Learn how the Weavers are co-creating Mancunianity Flows through different forms of knowledge, participation and choice.

Why “Weavers”?

The name recognises people who bring different threads together: places, work, journeys, music, family histories, observations and questions.

Co-creators, not a focus group

The Weavers can shape the route, challenge historical framing, choose material, test access, welcome audiences or work behind the scenes. No one is required to disclose personal history, speak publicly, perform, be photographed or be recorded.

Choice

People decide how, when and whether to contribute.

Different roles

Speaking, listening, writing, research, mapping, music, access testing and practical support all have value.

Public permission

Names, portraits, biographies, quotations and recordings will appear only when the relevant public use has been agreed.

Meet the people shaping the project

Tony Easom

Tony Easom is a Salford artist, folk musician and songwriter, a member of Salford Art Club and a former ship’s plumber. His memories of ships, tugs and locomotives around the River Irwell, the docks and the Manchester Ship Canal bring a distinctive working-life perspective to Mancunianity Flows.

Tony later worked at Manchester Dry Docks and on major industrial projects. Alongside that working life, he has continued to paint, photograph, perform and write songs rooted in people, place and public memory.

Among Tony’s earlier works is The Flag, also known as Who’ll Carry This Flag, which honours Jimmy Murphy. For Mancunianity Flows, Tony’s original words and music provide the foundations of a new River Irwell song. The material is being developed collaboratively through ten professionally led workshops at Aviva Studios, home of Factory International, involving Tony, the other Weavers, directors and musicians.
