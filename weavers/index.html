The Weavers

Learn how the Weavers are co-creating Mancunianity Flows through different forms of knowledge, participation and choice.

Why “Weavers”?

The name recognises people who bring different threads together: places, work, journeys, music, family histories, observations and questions. The project is being developed with a group of up to ten older local people.

Co-creators, not a focus group

The Weavers can shape the route, challenge historical framing, choose material, test access, welcome audiences or work behind the scenes. No one is required to disclose personal history, speak publicly, perform, be photographed or be recorded.

Choice

People decide how, when and whether to contribute.

Different roles

Speaking, listening, writing, research, mapping, music, access testing and practical support all have value.

Public permission

Names, portraits, biographies, quotations and recordings will appear only when the relevant public use has been agreed.

Tony Easom

Tony Easom, 76, is a Salford artist, folk musician and songwriter, active Salford Art Club member and former ship's plumber. His memories of ships, tugs and locomotives around the River Irwell, the docks and the Manchester Ship Canal bring a distinctive working-life perspective to Mancunianity Flows.

Tony later worked at Manchester Dry Docks and on major industrial projects. Alongside that working life, he has continued to paint, photograph, perform and write songs rooted in people, place and public memory.

Among Tony's earlier works is The Flag, also known as Who'll Carry This Flag, which honours Jimmy Murphy. For Mancunianity Flows, Tony's original words and music provide the foundations of a new River Irwell song. Across ten professionally led workshops at Aviva Studios, home of Factory International, Tony, the other Weavers, directors and musicians are developing the material collaboratively.

Tony is one of ten proud local Weavers aged 60 and over, drawn from Manchester and Salford and from both banks of the Irwell. Together, the Weavers bring working lives, memories, humour, local knowledge and creative ideas into the stories, route, music, access and audience experience.

On 19, 20 and 21 December 2026, the project aims to welcome people from both cities and across the region to promenade experiences on and beside the river. Each journey will culminate in a festive mini-concert, bringing distinctive voices and different generations together in one united chorus.

Mancunianity Flows is supported by Arts Council England and developed through ten professionally led workshops at Aviva Studios, home of Factory International.
