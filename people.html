Meet the people behind Mancunianity Flows

Created collaboratively. Led with experience.

Local people, artists and researchers are shaping a public experience where history, sound, memory and place meet.

Portrait: Michael Pollard Photography.

Creative leadership

Nakib Narat (he/him)

Creative Director, Producer, Playwright, Performer and Blue Badge Tourist Guide

Nakib Narat is a creative producer, playwright, performer, researcher and qualified Blue Badge Tourist Guide for the UK and Ireland. His work explores place, belonging, participation and urban identity.

The idea for Mancunianity Flows developed over two years through Nakib's artistic practice and extensive work with older people across Greater Manchester. Nakib founded and leads Chorlton Senior Drama Workshops, is Co-Chair of the Greater Manchester Older People's Equality Panel and serves on Manchester's Age Friendly Board.

Read more about Nakib's creative career

Mancunianity Flows is Nakib's second Arts Council England-supported award. His first supported Ellen's Longest Night, a professional theatre production about Mancunian politician Ellen Wilkinson.

Nakib originated and co-produced The Manchester Passion 2017, developed the script and performed Pontius Pilate for an audience of around 5,000 in Cathedral Gardens, with coverage on BBC One's Songs of Praise.

Before becoming a full-time actor, Nakib worked as a Granada Television journalist alongside Tony Wilson and later filmed three featured Coronation Street roles. Nakib later became a community producer with Aviva Studios, helped create The Welcome, and appeared in BBC One’s imagine… The Factory: Made in Manchester.

Dr Simon Knighton

Audio Associate, Composer and Sound Artist

Simon Knighton is an Ivor Novello Award-nominated composer and recipient of the Royal Musical Association's Tippett Medal. Based in Manchester, Simon completed a PhD at the Royal Northern College of Music.

Working across acoustic instruments, electronics and field recording, Simon has composed for ensembles across Europe. For Mancunianity Flows, Simon contributes recording, editing, sound design, composition, listening practices and headset preparation.

Live music and participation

Musician and Participatory Artist

Creative Facilitation, Audience Leadership and Musical Development

A vital creative voice in the event

The Musician and Participatory Artist is central to the character, warmth and musical life of Mancunianity Flows.

Working across music and participatory arts, the artist supports the Weavers' workshops, collaborative music-making, script development, live performance and family-friendly audience participation. The artist is developing the final concert, including the group song, sea shanty and songs connected to Manchester, Salford, work and waterways.

The artist is credited by role, with the focus on the vital creative contribution to the project and its audiences.

Co-creators at the heart of the project

The Weavers

The Weavers are local people aged 50+ who help shape the project's stories, questions, recordings, sounds, humour and audience experience. Their knowledge sits alongside researched history and professional artistic practice.

Many ways to contribute

Memories, questions, observations, recordings, rhythms, fact-checks and audience prompts can all become part of the work.

Choice comes first

No Weaver must perform, share private experiences, be recorded or appear in a photograph. Every public contribution is agreed and reviewed.

Co-creation, not consultation

The Weavers influence what audiences hear, notice, discuss and remember.

Our workshop home

Making the project at Aviva Studios

Aviva Studios, home of Factory International, has generously provided Mancunianity Flows with a workshop home and development base. Here, the Weavers and creative team meet, experiment with sound and music, exchange stories and shape the public experience together.

The venue's riverside location places the project's creative development directly within the landscape it explores.
