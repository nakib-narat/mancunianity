Creative Journey

The River Irwell is the spine, the transport web is the structure, and the Weavers help decide what the public encounters.

A co-created journey in developmentThe themes below are an introduction, not the complete performance or final self-guided route.

Older local Weavers are working alongside professional artists to explore memory, place, sound and change. Session outcomes are not predetermined: participants may alter, combine, challenge or reject ideas as the work develops.

1 • Starting the web

Aviva Studios and River Square

A place once shaped by freight and industry now brings together art, audiences and ideas. The project begins by asking where each person’s Manchester or Salford story starts.

Think about it: What makes a place become part of somebody’s story?

2 • River fact

Meet the River Irwell

The river predates the roads, canals and railways around it. It has carried work and trade, absorbed serious pollution and remains a living waterway under continuing pressure.

Look closely: Notice water, movement, reflections, plants and the hard edges created by the city.

3 • Look closely

Bridges through time

Different generations of engineering occupy the same view. Historic railway fabric, a modern rail connection and a pedestrian crossing show how movement continues to reshape the river corridor.

Compare: Which materials, shapes or details suggest that one structure is older than another?

4 • Follow the thread

The diligent spider

In 1844, French observer Léon Faucher compared Manchester to a diligent spider at the centre of a great web. The project extends that image beyond roads and railways to include migration, friendship, care, memory and chance encounters.

Think about it: Which human connections make a city liveable?

5 • History mystery

Brick, air and railway work

Patterned brickwork allowed air through enclosed railway spaces. Project research is continuing into how particular spaces were used and how horses fitted into the working railway landscape.

Steam moved heavy loads over distance, but horses still handled carts and precise short movements in busy urban yards. The railway depended on both technology and skilled animal labour.

Look closely: Why build a secure wall that also lets air pass through?

6 • Between two cities

Crossing the Irwell

Crossing changes the point of view. Manchester becomes something to look back towards, while the river continues below and trains pass through the wider landscape.

Listen: What can you hear before you can see its source?

7 • Then and now

Living inside the web

Networks carried coal, cotton, food, letters and people, but city history is more than infrastructure. Work, family, music, neighbourhood and belonging give the web its human meaning.

Think about it: Can we value a place and still want it to change?

8 • Coming soon

Add another thread

The live experience, approved audio, schools resources and future walk guide will reveal more. Participant material will appear only with the appropriate permission.
